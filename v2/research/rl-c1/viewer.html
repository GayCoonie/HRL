Same addresses, not a simulated blur. Each pixel is produced through that model’s inverse transform. White is upper-left, black lower-left, and vivid at the right vertex. The new atlas has monotone cubic path interpolation and convex cubic secondary interpolation; its physical hue sheets, ring, vivid anchors, and A Smooth neutral progression are held fixed. Previews are quantized to display sRGB, but all benchmark calculations used continuous XYZ.

The ramp check measures neighboring Oklab step-size jumps. It is a model-based visual diagnostic, not an observer experiment. “Less abrupt” is not the same as perfectly even spacing: the balanced candidate’s overall ramp CV is slightly worse than A Smooth, although better than 0.8A. Some ColorBench threshold scores also regress. This remains a review candidate, not a replacement release.

Benchmark coverage and provenance

The baseline ran ColorBench’s five scored generation datasets and sixteen scored measurement columns. Unsupported colors were never squeezed into gamut: the bounded models cannot score all Hung–Berns stimuli or the complete Brown-1957 neighborhoods. Partial-support scores are flagged in the report; no whole-board rank is claimed. No unscored appearance diagnostics or application scenarios were run.
